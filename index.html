RehArt research · Participant data collection

Understanding stroke recovery in everyday hospital life.

StrokeRehart is a data-collection application supporting the RehArt research project. The study explores whether a more stimulating and engaging inpatient rehabilitation environment — combining meaningful activities, music and opportunities for social participation alongside standard care — can support recovery after stroke.

The research context

More than scheduled therapy.

RehArt investigates the role of enriched environments in inpatient stroke rehabilitation. Alongside the usual multidisciplinary rehabilitation programme, the research examines whether structured opportunities for physical activity, leisure, music and social engagement can make the hospital stay more active and meaningful.

Standard care

The usual multidisciplinary inpatient rehabilitation programme, including physiotherapy, occupational therapy and speech and language therapy.

Increased physical activity

Standard care plus additional daily individual physical exercises.

Enriched environment

Standard care complemented by an enriched programme of meaningful activities and engagement, including music-based and leisure activities.

01 · Admission

Baseline assessment at the start of the inpatient rehabilitation period.

02 · Discharge

Post-intervention assessment after the three-week programme.

03 · Follow-up

A further assessment one month after discharge.

The research aims to evaluate daily functioning and recovery, motor, cognitive and emotional outcomes, quality of life, participant and professional experiences, and the feasibility and cost-effectiveness of the approach. The study is a randomized controlled trial aiming to recruit 90 stroke survivors in the subacute phase. It is conducted at the neurological rehabilitation unit of Hospital de l’Esperança (Hospital del Mar), Barcelona.

About the application

A research data-collection tool.

The StrokeRehart application helps organise selected health information that participants choose to share through Google Health. It supports the research team's work by collecting and presenting relevant measures over time, complementing the study's clinical assessments and other research data.

Participant-authorised access

Connection to Google Health takes place through Google's authorisation process. Participants control the permissions they grant.

Selected measures

The current integration is configured for activity and fitness, health metrics and measurements, and sleep data, according to the permissions requested.

Research support

Collected information is organised for the study's data-collection and analysis workflows and is made available to authorised project personnel.

The application is a research data-collection tool. It does not provide a diagnosis, recommend or deliver treatment, monitor emergencies, or replace clinical assessments and professional medical advice. Use of the application is subject to the study information and participant consent process.

Data and transparency

How information is used.

StrokeRehart accesses Google Health data only after authorisation and uses it for the research purpose explained to participants. The exact data categories, recipients, storage arrangements, retention periods and participant-rights procedures are described in the Privacy Policy and the study information provided during enrolment.

The use of information received from Google Health API and/or Developer Tools will adhere to the Google Health API Developer and User Data Policy and the Google Health API User Data and Health Research Policy, including the applicable Limited Use requirements.

Please read our Privacy Policy for information about data collection, use, storage, sharing, retention and deletion. The website does not replace the participant-facing information, informed consent, ethics approvals or research-policy requirements applicable to the study.

The wider project

Researching better rehabilitation experiences.

StrokeRehart is connected to the RehArt research line, which brings together clinicians and researchers to study how enriched rehabilitation environments can support recovery, participation and well-being after stroke.
